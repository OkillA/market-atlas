# Critical content and reader-experience audit — 2026-10-02

Reviewer: `/root`. Requested scope: content first, then UI, UX, layout, colors and the rest of the reader experience. This is a review, not an implementation pass. The actionable queue is [FEEDBACK.md](FEEDBACK.md); original findings and prior dispositions remain in [FEEDBACK_RESOLVED.md](FEEDBACK_RESOLVED.md).

## Assessment

The manuscript is substantially more careful and more complete than the initial audit's draft. Its strongest features are explicit information cutoffs, reproducible calculations, failed and skipped paths, cost-aware accounting, and limits on empirical inference. The examples generally distinguish what happened, what was modeled and what could actually have been known. Those features should survive revision.

The principal remaining weakness is the learning experience. The book often explains an answer before labeling a closely related task “independent,” asks for a prospective decision while showing its continuation, and presents long reference material through navigation designed for short chapters. There are also specific correctness defects: a wrong worked solution, contradictory holding-session language and a numerical rounding warning that its own values do not demonstrate. These matter even though every documented numerical/schema check passes.

The visual identity is coherent: warm paper, dark green, serif prose and restrained decoration suit a serious handbook. Main prose is readable. Supporting information receives too little visual weight: small type, marginal text contrast, unkeyed chart legends and constrained tables make the evidence and qualifications harder to use than the headlines. The interface has demonstrated accessibility and recovery failures, rather than merely needing cosmetic polish.

**Recommended next work:** repair content contradictions and recoverability/access defects first; then improve independent practice and staged cases; then make chapter/evidence navigation and essential tables usable; finally refine the visual hierarchy. Adding more indicators, more caveats or a wholesale visual redesign would not address the demonstrated problems.

## Content review

### Correctness that needs immediate attention

**Chapter 6's guided solution is for a different question (CA1-015).** The prompt supplies resistance peaks 55.10, 54.90 and 55.30 with a 0.20 upward buffer. Its answer discusses support at 39.80–40.35 and a downward threshold of 39.50. The requested resistance answer is zone 54.90–55.30, threshold 55.50, then visit / edge crossing / buffered break for 55.20 / 55.40 / 55.60. A reader checking correct work receives an unrelated explanation. The immediately following independent task repeats the same resistance inputs. A nonempty solution and passing arithmetic assertions did not catch this pairing failure.

**Chapter 24 uses two time-exit clocks (AUD2-002).** “Entry day counts as holding session 1” makes the tenth close inclusive of entry. “Tenth session after entry” normally means ten subsequent sessions. The same specification should not allow both interpretations. Chapter 26's “longest possible holding window” additionally needs qualification because its halt rules admit delayed exits. This is a precision defect in an otherwise useful inspectable capstone, not evidence that the supplied cash ledger is arithmetically wrong.

**The MSFT near-boundary warning is an invalid example (AUD2-001).** Lower boundary 51.82353 rounds ordinarily to 51.82. Open 51.830 is above either boundary. The claimed transition from intrabar to opening classification therefore does not happen under nearest-cent rounding. Preserve the correct historical classification; revise the teaching claim or provide a separate example with a named rounding policy that actually changes it.

**The isolated R definition loses an important convention (CA1-024).** The chapters distinguish maximum order risk, actual filled-position price-risk and cost-inclusive loss. “Original planned dollar risk” alone is too ambiguous for this book's glossary. The denominator and numerator convention should be recoverable without rereading several chapters.

### Assessment and independent transfer

The revised quizzes use materially better distractors than the original audit described. Wrong denominators, timestamps, price fields and initialization choices are worthwhile diagnostic errors. Nevertheless, the answer locations now create a shortcut: second option is correct in 20/28 chapters and every one of Chapters 20–28. A reader can score 71.4% by selecting position two throughout. Rebalance positions while preserving the explanations; do not replace good questions with decorative randomness (CA1-016).

Guided and independent practice need a clearer distinction. Reusing the same data can help rehearsal, but it does not by itself demonstrate transfer. Chapter 10 repeats already-explained range switches; Chapter 13 repeats the last-price/POC/VWAP result; Chapter 17 repeats final spot/futures comparisons; Chapter 19 repeats the 100-of-250 fill. Fresh-input tasks already exist in Chapters 14, 20 and 25. Apply that pattern selectively where an “independent” label currently overstates the task (additional evidence on CA1-015).

Prospective case reading is the right instructional method, but the interface does not enforce the intended order. Chapter 28's headings name the outcome; the completed calendar ledger and future paths are already visible. “Cover the next rows” places the burden on the learner to avoid information the page presents directly. A cutoff-first learner view, a continuation reveal and a subsequent reconciliation view would make the author's method concrete. Keep all selection/provenance information available in a reference view; do not hide inconvenient cases (AUD2-003).

Human learning remains unmeasured. Prepared forms and this audit are useful, but they are not evidence that investing-literate beginners can independently perform the intended outcomes. CA1-026 stays deferred; nothing here invents participants or replaces that study with model opinions.

## UI, UX and visual design

### Navigation and reference use

The desktop sidebar is calm and consistent, and direct chapter routes work. The design's main weakness is long-form reading navigation. Mobile loses the section list altogether. Desktop retains it but does not constrain its height: Chapter 28's final link is below the 900px viewport even when the aside is sticky. Section URLs do not retain chapter identity, and resumption retains only chapter identity without section position. Together, these choices make revisiting an exact calculation surprisingly laborious (AUD2-007, 008, 015, 016).

Search feels simple but has an incomplete model of the book. It ignores much of the instructional/reference material and sends matches to chapter tops. The evidence appendix has 175 records and no navigable index/filter. A book built around inspecting claims should expose that work as a primary reader destination, with contextual access (AUD2-006, 014).

The six part reviews and four playground routes render successfully. The risk tool rejects a stop above entry and states its no-cost/no-leverage assumptions; no defect is logged solely because it is a deliberately simplified calculator. Chapter-to-playground linking can be much more useful by selecting the relevant existing experiment (AUD2-019).

### Layout and typography

Keep the main text measure and serif body. The large title, takeaway and learning-outcome box create clear entry hierarchy. Supporting material needs more emphasis: 10–12px metadata, source notes and contents links; 8–9px kickers; compact figure values often below the prose size by a large margin. Increase the important small type before reducing whitespace or adding decoration (AUD2-012).

Nested 280px table scroll areas are particularly unsuitable for central ledgers. On the phone, the complete calendar ledger initially shows about two rows, clips its right-hand caption and loses headers when scrolled. The learner must compare records through a narrow slot. Essential tables should be expandable and keep column context; optional raw data can remain compact (AUD2-009).

The prose-only reading-time estimate is not inherently wrong, but the label should disclose what it measures. Table inspection and independent exercises are a large part of the intended learning session, especially in the later chapters. Reader studies can eventually inform realistic ranges; this audit supplies no measured completion times (AUD2-017).

Chapter chart series names appear as an unkeyed gray sentence below green/rust/gold paths. The SMA and EMA should not require looking up code or reconstructing numbers just to identify their lines. Add swatches and redundant line styles/labels; harmonize the browser's gold token with the different exported SVG palette. Finite geometry checks did not catch flow titles extending past their SVG bounds at 320px (AUD2-004, 011, 018).

### Accessibility, printing and reader data

The `#main` skip link is actively harmful: the hash router replaces the current chapter with an error page. This should be corrected before general accessibility polish (AUD2-005). The route/extent audit is not a screen-reader audit or full WCAG certification; no such claim is made.

The print pathway hides answer options and leaves constrained table overflow in place. Exact CSS simulation confirms missing controls and clipped data geometry, but real pagination still requires inspection. Define a useful learner/answer print policy rather than leaving output dependent on which disclosures happened to be open (AUD2-010).

Notebook export/import is a real recoverability concern. The UI permits 50,000 units of text per chapter, but import imposes a smaller aggregate byte ceiling than some valid multilingual exports. A 15-chapter, individually valid constructed payload was rejected at 2,250,472 bytes. Fix the contract, not just that particular test payload (AUD2-013).
